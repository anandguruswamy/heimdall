HEIMDALL

Room-wide radar mesh, from $30 radios

UWB radios were invented to answer one question — how far away is the key fob?
But every ping bounces off the whole room. Our hack: build a radar mesh out of five UWB radios.

Built by five Qualcomm UWB / Bluetooth modem engineers · Anand · Ehsan · Jianxiu · Saisundar · Simarjit

01 · Why UWB

Every ranging radio is secretly a radar.

01

A UWB radio fires a short pulse. It bounces off walls, furniture, and people — and the radio records every echo.

02

Pulses are just a few nanoseconds wide, so each echo is separated in time — a full reflected-energy profile of the room.

03

No cameras. Works in the dark, through smoke and non-metal walls — and it records where things are, never what you look like.

Today the industry uses it for one thing — IS THE KEY FOB IN RANGE?  →  the least interesting measurement it makes.

02 · The Hack

Five key-fob rangers → a radar mesh.

We replaced the vendor firmware with our own bespoke protocol on open Zephyr RTOS — every node transmits and listens.

Radio mesh

5 × DWM3001 · 35 ms cycles · custom beacon protocol

Node 0 Gateway

Qualcomm QRB2210 · validate · archive · DSP · serve

Dashboard

live dashboard web app

Heimdall beacon protocolround-robin · one TX per slot

Every ping works double duty

  • All nodes capture a fresh CIR from each ping
  • Each ping's payload carries telemetry — node 0 reports it, peers sync
  • One ping, many new CIRs
  • All-to-all CIR collection at maximum rate
